\subsection{A finite model without transitivity}\label{sec:finite-model}

Take $X=\{0,1\}^3$, with identity evolution and coarse-graining,
noiseless records and preparation $x_0=(0,0,0)$. One trial suffices;
detection means unequal records. Write $A(a,b,c)=a$, $B(a,b,c)=b$
and $C(a,b,c)=c$. Admit exactly the identity and the four maps
\begin{align}
 U_{A=\alpha}(a,b,c)&=(\alpha,\alpha,0),\label{eq:model-int-a}\\
 U_{B=\beta}(a,b,c)&=(a,\beta,a\oplus\beta),\label{eq:model-int-b}
\end{align}
where $\oplus$ is exclusive or. Interventions set their named event
on every state, not only on the preparation. In this model the maps
propagate $b=a$ and $c=a\oplus b$ after setting $A$, but preserve
$a$ after setting $B$.
\leandef{MonadFormal.Profile,MonadFormal.Interv,MonadFormal.Influences,MonadFormal.model}

\begin{theorem}[Direct influence need not be transitive]
\label{thm:influence-nontransitivity}
In this model, $A\DirectInfluence B$ and $B\DirectInfluence C$, but
$A\not\DirectInfluence C$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The pair $U_{A=1},U_{A=0}$ changes the $B$ record from one to zero.
The pair $U_{B=1},U_{B=0}$ similarly changes the $C$ record at $x_0$.
Neither the identity nor either $U_{B=\beta}$ sets $A$ constantly,
so the only admissible interventions on $A$ are $U_{A=0}$ and
$U_{A=1}$. Both return $C=0$. Thus no alternative admitted pair
can witness influence from $A$ to $C$.
\leanproof{MonadFormal.influences_A_B,MonadFormal.influences_B_C,MonadFormal.interv_A_eq,MonadFormal.not_influences_A_C}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic protocol monotonicity]
\label{thm:influence-monotonicity}
For noiseless deterministic profiles with the same preparation, if
every operation of one profile is admitted by the other, every
direct influence detected by the former is detected by the latter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Reuse the witnessing intervention pair. Both operations remain admitted,
their event-setting equalities are unchanged, and the same preparation
gives the same unequal records.
\leanproof{MonadFormal.influences_mono}
\end{proof}
The scope matters: with noisy channels, trial limits or changed
thresholds, operation-set inclusion alone is insufficient. The full
protocol contract must preserve the entire detection witness.
