\section{A fixed-context observer profile}\label{sec:introduction}

The preceding sections isolate several pressures one at a time:
bounded continuation, obtainable repair, approximation, separating
counterexamples, and temporal dependence. MONAD-FORC now freezes a
single context and asks what follows when state, resources, records,
interventions, and information bounds must coexist.

MONAD-FORC~0.2 is that fixed-context profile. Its state, observation,
intervention-influence, entropy, and information structures
instantiate part of the programme of \Cref{sec:problem-statement}
without identifying the programme with one observer model.

The profile begins with a specific mathematical commitment: a regulated closed-world state space
$\StateSpace$ equipped with a reference measure $\Measure$ and invertible,
measure-preserving evolution $\Evolution$
(\Cref{sec:state-space}). With the stated integrability condition,
fine-grained entropy is conserved; this baseline supplies no intrinsic
thermodynamic arrow.

The observer interface (\Cref{sec:observer}) introduces explicit
information-losing operations even though the closed baseline evolution
is reversible: a coarse-graining map
$\CoarseGrain$ and a noisy record channel $\RecordChannel$, both indexed by a
finite resource vector $\ResourceVec$.  The loss channels are explicit
rather than exclusive: an allowed operation need not be invertible, and
an injective coarse-graining loses nothing.  The set of allowed operations
$\AllowedOps$ is also resource-bounded.

Operational causality (\Cref{sec:operational-causality}) is then
captured by resource-indexed detectable intervention influence
$\DirectInfluence$: event $A$ detectably influences event $B$ when an
allowed intervention on $A$ produces a detectable change in the
reliable-record distribution of $B$, where detectability is measured by
a divergence $\Distinguishability$ exceeding a threshold $\eta$.  The
relation is not assumed to be a preorder; transitivity fails in a
finite worked model (\Cref{sec:finite-model}).

\Cref{sec:entropy-arrows} addresses entropy and thermodynamic arrows.
Macro-entropy is a functional of the chosen coarse-graining, and monotonic
increase is \emph{not} automatic from reversibility alone; it requires
additional boundary-condition and typicality assumptions.

Finally, \Cref{sec:information-bounds} develops information processing,
single-letter capacity and binary testing. The finite-alphabet Chernoff
exponent, moving-support example and scalar local distance expansions
have separate hypotheses. The operational coding interpretation is
distinguished from the single-letter capacity functional.

Together, these five layers (state space, observer, causality, entropy
arrows, and information bounds) form MONAD-FORC~0.2.  They do not form
MONAD itself: the profile is one fixed-context instance of the
programme of \Cref{sec:problem-statement}.  The correspondence is
stated next.
